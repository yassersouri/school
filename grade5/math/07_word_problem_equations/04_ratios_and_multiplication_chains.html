✖️ "Times as Many" Means Multiply
"A has 3 times as many as B" means: A = 3 × B. If B = 5, then A = 15.

This is also called a ratio — it compares two amounts by multiplication instead of by "how much more".
Example: A has 4 times as many stickers as B

The fraction flip trick 🔄

🔄 "A Fraction Of" — Flip It Around!
Sometimes a clue is given "backwards", using a fraction: "C has one third of D's money." This means C is the smaller one. To compare the other way, flip the fraction into a multiplication:

"C is 1/3 of D"  ⟺  "D is 3 times C"   (D = 3 × C)
Why Does This Work?
If D = 30, and C is one third of D, then C = 30 ÷ 3 = 10.
Check the flip: is D = 3 × C?   3 × 10 = 30 = D ✓   Yes! Flipping a "1/n of" clue always gives "n times".

Putting all three clues together 🌟

🌟 Three Links in the Chain
The "Money Chains" challenge puzzles give you three clues connecting four people (A, B, C, D). Chain them all the way from A down to D: multiply every ratio, and flip any fractions first.
Worked Example
Story: A has 3 times as much as B. B has 2 times as much as C. C has one half of D. How many times more is A than D?

Step 1 — flip the fraction: "C is 1/2 of D" → D = 2 × C
Step 2 — chain forward: A = 3B, and B = 2C, so A = 3 × 2 × C = 6C
Step 3 — swap in D: since D = 2C, we know C = D ÷ 2, so A = 6 × (D ÷ 2) = 3 × D
A has 3 times as much as D. (Shortcut: multiply 3 × 2 = 6, then divide by 2 = 3.)
