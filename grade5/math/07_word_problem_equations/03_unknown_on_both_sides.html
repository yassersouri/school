📦 A New Kind of Equation
So far, x has only shown up on one side of the equation. But sometimes x shows up on both sides — like x + 9 = 2x. This says "a number, plus 9, equals double that same number."

The trick: think of x as a box of unknown weight. Both sides have boxes — and boxes on both sides of a balanced scale can be removed in matching pairs, just like removing the same weight from both sides!
Example: x + 9 = 2x

Both sides have 1 matching box. Cross out one box from each side — it's the same as subtracting x from both sides:
x + 9 − x = 2x − x  →  9 = x
Check: 9 + 9 = 18, and 2 × 9 = 18 ✓
💡 Big idea: When x is on both sides, always move all the x's to ONE side first (by subtracting the smaller amount of x from both sides). Then it becomes a one-step or two-step equation you already know how to solve!

Worked Word Problem
Story: If we add 20 to a number, the sum is 3 times that number. What is the number?

Let x = the mystery number.
Equation: x + 20 = 3x
Cancel 1 box: 20 = 2x  →  x = 20 ÷ 2 = 10
Check: 10 + 20 = 30, and 3 × 10 = 30 ✓
🔀 Same Idea, Different Words
This exact same type of puzzle can be worded in different ways. They all lead to the same kind of equation!
Variant: Subtracting Instead of Adding
Story: If we subtract 6 from a number, the result is half of that number.
Equation: x − 6 = x ÷ 2. Multiply both sides by 2 to clear the fraction: 2x − 12 = x
Cancel 1 box: −12 = x − 2x  →  rearrange: x = 12
Check: 12 − 6 = 6, and 12 ÷ 2 = 6 ✓
Variant: "Difference" Wording
Story: The difference between 4 times a number and the number itself is 21.
Equation: 4x − x = 21  →  3x = 21  →  x = 7
Check: 4 × 7 − 7 = 28 − 7 = 21 ✓   This is really the same as x + 21 = 4x!
