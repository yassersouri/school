🍊🍎 Two Mystery Prices, Two Clues
Some problems have two unknowns at once — like the price of oranges AND the price of apples. One clue isn't enough to solve it, but two clues (two different shopping trips) are! The trick: line the two clues up and find a way to cancel one unknown out.
Worked Example — When a Coefficient Already Matches
Clue 1: 2 kg oranges + 2 kg apples = $14
Clue 2: 5 kg oranges + 2 kg apples = $23

Both clues have "2 kg apples" — the apple part is identical! So if we subtract Clue 1 from Clue 2, the apples cancel out completely:
(5 kg oranges + 2 kg apples) − (2 kg oranges + 2 kg apples) = 23 − 14
3 kg oranges = $9  →  1 kg orange = $3
Back-substitute into Clue 1: 2(3) + 2 kg apples = 14 → 2 kg apples = 8 → 1 kg apple = $4
Always check: 5(3) + 2(4) = 15 + 8 = 23 ✓ matches Clue 2!
💡 The Big Idea: Subtracting two true equations gives another true equation. If a part matches exactly on both sides, it disappears — leaving just ONE unknown to solve!

📐 What If Nothing Matches Yet?
Usually the two clues won't match right away. But if one coefficient is a clean multiple of the other, we can multiply the whole clue by a number to force a match — just like scaling a recipe up!
Worked Example — Scale One Clue
Clue 1: 3 kg oranges + 2 kg apples = $18
Clue 2: 2 kg oranges + 4 kg apples = $20

Notice: Clue 2's apple amount (4) is exactly double Clue 1's apple amount (2). So multiply all of Clue 1 by 2:
Clue 1 × 2:   6 kg oranges + 4 kg apples = $36
Now both clues have "4 kg apples" — subtract Clue 2 from the new Clue 1:
(6o + 4a) − (2o + 4a) = 36 − 20  →  4o = 16  →  o = $4
Back-substitute into Clue 1: 3(4) + 2a = 18 → 12 + 2a = 18 → 2a = 6 → a = $3
Check Clue 2: 2(4) + 4(3) = 8 + 12 = 20 ✓
💡 Remember: Whatever you multiply on one side of an equation, multiply on every term — otherwise the equation is no longer true!

🔢 When Neither Side Divides Evenly
Sometimes neither coefficient is a multiple of the other. Then scale both clues — just like finding a common denominator — so a matching coefficient appears on both.
Worked Example — Scale Both Clues
Clue 1: 3 kg grapes + 2 kg pears = $24
Clue 2: 2 kg grapes + 3 kg pears = $26

Neither 3 nor 2 divides the other evenly. But both divide into 6. Scale Clue 1 by 2, and Clue 2 by 3:
Clue 1 × 2:   6 grapes + 4 pears = $48
Clue 2 × 3:   6 grapes + 9 pears = $78
Subtract (bigger − smaller) so grapes cancel:
(6g + 9p) − (6g + 4p) = 78 − 48  →  5p = 30  →  p = $6
Back-substitute into Clue 1: 3g + 2(6) = 24 → 3g = 12 → g = $4
Check Clue 2: 2(4) + 3(6) = 8 + 18 = 26 ✓
