🔁 A Repeating Pattern
Some problems describe a number that gets smaller, then a bit bigger, then smaller again — over and over — following the same two steps each time. The question asks: how many steps until we hit exactly 0?
Warm-Up Example
Start at 20. Subtract 8, then add 4. Repeat. How many steps until we reach 0?
20 −8→ 12 +4→ 16 −8→ 8 +4→ 12 −8→ 4 +4→ 8 −8→ 0 🎉
Counting every arrow: that's 7 steps total, and we land exactly on 0 right after a subtraction.
💡 Why not just list every step? For small numbers, listing works fine! But if the answer takes 49 steps, listing them all would take forever — and it's easy to miscount. We need a faster, smarter way.
🔎 The Shortcut: Watch Only the "After-Subtract" Numbers
Look only at the values right after each subtraction (ignore the "add" steps in between): 12, 8, 4, 0.

Notice something neat: each one is exactly 4 less than the one before (that's 8 − 4, the subtract amount minus the add amount). This is a simple shrinking pattern — and we can count how many subtractions it takes to hit 0 without writing out the "add" steps at all!
Using the Shortcut
Step 1 — find the shrink amount: subtract − add = 8 − 4 = 4
Step 2 — find the first after-subtract value: 20 − 8 = 12
Step 3 — label every after-subtract value with its subtraction number, counting down by the shrink amount until you hit 0:
12 (sub #1) 8 (sub #2) 4 (sub #3) 0 (sub #4)
The label on the 0 tells us there were 4 subtractions in total.
Step 4 — total steps = subtractions + adds. There's always one fewer "add" than "subtract" (we stop right after the last subtraction): 4 subtractions + 3 adds = 7 steps ✓ matches our full list above!
⚠️ Careful — a common mistake: Count how many numbers are in the after-subtract list (that's the number of subtractions), not how many arrows connect them! The list "12, 8, 4, 0" has 4 numbers (4 subtractions), even though there are only 3 arrows between them.

🧮 Count the Numbers in the List, Then the Total Steps
Write out the after-subtract values, counting down by the shrink amount, until you reach 0. How many numbers are in that list (including the 0) — that's the number of subtractions. Since the pattern always ends right after a subtraction, the number of adds is always one less.

Total steps = subtractions + adds = subtractions + (subtractions − 1)
