# Grade 5 Math — Learning Path

This learning path organizes the Grade 5 material currently available in this
repository. It is a focused collection, not a complete Grade 5 curriculum.
Expectations vary by province and country; adapt terminology and pacing to the
learner's program.

## Before starting

Check that the learner can:

- recall multiplication and division facts through 10 × 10;
- add and subtract whole numbers using place value;
- explain a fraction as equal parts of one whole;
- identify the numerator and denominator;
- measure length with standard units; and
- find the area of a rectangle by counting squares or multiplying side lengths.

If a prerequisite is not secure, review it before using a timed or mixed sheet.

## Recommended sequence

### 1. Decimal addition and subtraction

**Goal:** Add and subtract decimals to hundredths by aligning place values and
explain why placeholder zeroes do not change a value.

Start with tenths, continue to hundredths, then use mixed calculations and word
problems.

📂 [Decimal Addition & Subtraction](01_decimal_addition_subtraction/index.html)

**Success check:** At least 8 of 10 calculations are accurate, decimal points
are aligned by place value, and the learner can estimate whether an answer is
reasonable.

### 2. Decimal multiplication

**Goal:** Multiply decimals using whole-number facts, place-value reasoning, and
estimation.

Begin with a decimal multiplied by a whole number. Continue to tenths by tenths
and hundredths only after the learner can predict the size of the product.

📂 [Decimal Multiplication](02_decimal_multiplication/index.html)

**Success check:** The learner estimates first, calculates accurately, and can
explain why multiplying by 10, 100, or 1,000 changes each digit's place value.

### 3. Fractions

**Goal:** Represent and compare fractions, convert among familiar forms, and
solve fraction calculations appropriate to the learner's local curriculum.

The current resource is a broad exam rather than an introductory lesson. Review
each included operation separately before using it as an assessment. Fraction
multiplication and division may be enrichment in some Grade 5 programs.

📂 [Fractions](03_fractions/index.html)

**Success check:** The learner shows work, uses equivalent fractions when
needed, simplifies where appropriate, and checks whether each result is
reasonable.

### 4. Area of shapes

**Goal:** Derive and use area formulas by connecting each formula to arrays,
rectangles, or rearranged shapes.

Study square and rectangle area first, then parallelograms, triangles, and
shaded regions. Always include square units.

📂 [Area of Shapes](06_area_of_shapes/index.html)

**Success check:** The learner selects the relevant measurements, labels units,
and explains how a parallelogram or triangle relates to a rectangle.

### 5. Variables and formulas

**Goal:** Treat a variable as a quantity that can change and substitute known
values into simple formulas.

Use patterns before symbolic formulas. Connect area formulas to the preceding
unit.

📂 [Understanding Formulas](05_understanding_formulas/index.html)

**Success check:** The learner identifies what each symbol represents,
substitutes with units, and explains the result in words.

### 6. Mathematical reasoning and proof

**Goal:** Make a claim, support it with examples or a general argument, and use
a counterexample to disprove an over-broad claim.

This is an enrichment unit. Begin with counterexamples and picture proofs.
Even/odd arguments, cases, and if–then reasoning should follow only when the
language and notation are comfortable.

📂 [Introduction to Proof](04_intro_to_proof/index.html)

**Success check:** The learner distinguishes an example from a proof, states
what has been shown, and can revise a false claim after finding a
counterexample.

## Assessment guidance

- Use tutorials and worked examples during learning; use mixed sheets after
  skills are secure.
- Accept diagrams, equations, and written explanations when they communicate
  valid reasoning.
- Review errors by type: concept, operation, place value, units, or reading.
- Do not combine scores from unrelated topics into a single mastery judgment.
