💡 What Is a Parallelogram?
A parallelogram is a 4-sided shape where opposite sides are parallel and equal. A rectangle is a special parallelogram with right angles — but most parallelograms lean to the side.

We pick one side as the base (b). The height (h) is the perpendicular distance from the base to the opposite side — straight up, not along the slanted side!
Rectangle vs. Parallelogram
The blue dashed line is the height — always perpendicular to the base, never the red slanted side!
⚠️ Watch out: If a problem also tells you the length of the slanted side, don't use it for the area! Only the perpendicular height belongs in the formula.

✂️ Cut and Slide → Rectangle!
Cut a triangle off one side of the parallelogram, slide it to the other side, and you get a rectangle with the same base and height!
So the parallelogram has the same area as a rectangle with length b and width h.
The Cut-and-Slide Trick

→
Same base, same height, same area — it's just rearranged! That's why area = b × h.
Area of a parallelogram Area = base × height = b × h
Worked Example
Find the area of a parallelogram with base 9 cm and height 5 cm:
Step 1: Write the formula → Area = b × h
Step 2: Plug in → 9 × 5
Step 3: Calculate → 45 cm² ✓

2 Spot the Trick 🕵️
Each problem gives extra information. Pick the right base and height!
  • A parallelogram has base 10 cm, height 6 cm, and slanted side 7 cm. Area = cm² (don't use the 7!)
  • A parallelogram has base 12 m, height 5 m, and slanted side 8 m. Area = m²
  • A parallelogram has base 15 cm, slanted side 10 cm, and height 4 cm. Area = cm²
3 Find the Missing Number 🔍
Area = b × h, so b = Area ÷ h and h = Area ÷ b.
Area = 40 cm², b = 8 cm → h = cm
Area = 54 m², h = 6 m → b = m
