🎨 Area of Shaded Regions

Lesson 5 — The big idea: subtract the hole! 🕳️

Page 1 of 3
💡 What's a "Shaded Region"?
Sometimes a picture has a colored (shaded) part and a white (unshaded) part. You're asked: "What is the area of the shaded part?"

The trick: don't try to measure the weird shaded shape directly. Instead, work with the simple shapes hiding inside the picture!
📖 Recipe: Subtract the Hole
  1. Find the area of the big outer shape.
  2. Find the area of the white (unshaded) shape inside.
  3. Subtract: Shaded = Big − White.
Shaded Area = Outer Area − Inner Area
Worked Example: Rectangle with a Square Hole
A 20 cm × 10 cm rectangle has a 6 cm × 6 cm square cut out. Find the shaded area.
Step 1: Outer area = 20 × 10 = 200 cm²
Step 2: Inner area = 6 × 6 = 36 cm²
Step 3: Shaded = 200 − 36 = 164 cm² ✓

💡 When the Shape Is Made of Pieces
Sometimes the shaded region is built up from simpler shapes — like an L-shape made from two rectangles. Then you don't subtract — you add!
📖 Recipe: Add the Pieces
  1. Split the shape into rectangles, squares, triangles, or parallelograms.
  2. Find the area of each piece.
  3. Add them together.
Worked Example: An L-Shape
An L-shape splits into rectangle A (8 cm × 6 cm on top) and rectangle B (16 cm × 6 cm on bottom).
Step 1: Area of A = 8 × 6 = 48 cm²
Step 2: Area of B = 16 × 6 = 96 cm²
Step 3: Total shaded = 48 + 96 = 144 cm² ✓
💡 Tip: Sometimes either method works! You can split an L-shape into 2 rectangles (add) OR you can imagine the full big rectangle and subtract the missing corner (subtract). Try both and check your answer matches!
