💡 A Variable Is a Placeholder
In math, a variable is a letter (like n, x, or a) that stands for a number. It's like a blank box — you can put different numbers into it!

When someone writes n + 3, they mean: "Pick any number, call it n, and add 3 to it."
Example: What Does n + 3 Mean?
n = 1
→
1 + 3 = 4
n = 5
→
5 + 3 = 8
n = 10
→
10 + 3 = 13
Every time you replace n with a number, you get a new answer!

✖️ Multiplying with Variables
When you see 2 × n (or just 2n), it means "double the number."
When you see 3 × n (or 3n), it means "triple the number." The number next to the letter tells you how many times to multiply!

🧩 Two Steps: Multiply, Then Add or Subtract
Sometimes a formula has two operations. For example:
2 × n + 1 means "double n, then add 1."
2 × n − 1 means "double n, then subtract 1."

Always do the multiplication first, then the addition or subtraction.
Example: 2 × n − 1
nStep 1: 2 × nStep 2: subtract 1Result
12 × 1 = 22 − 11
22 × 2 = 44 − 13
32 × 3 = 66 − 15
The results are 1, 3, 5 — odd numbers! Keep going: n=4→7, n=5→9. The formula 2n − 1 gives you the nth odd number.
