# Grade 2 Writing — Comprehensive Syllabus

A complete year-long writing syllabus aimed at a Canadian Grade 2 level (ages 7–8). This is a new subject area in the repo, expanding Grade 1's early **Writing Connection** work in `../reading/syllabus.md` into a full writing curriculum with direct instruction in sentence building, grammar, paragraph structure, and short forms of composition. Links to existing repo materials are included as 📂 references; topics not yet built are marked **(needs exercises)** for future development.

The year is organized into four terms. Pacing can stay flexible so students have time to plan, talk, draft, revise, and publish with confidence.

> **Curriculum alignment note:** Cross-checked against the official **BC Ministry of Education English Language Arts curriculum** (curriculum.gov.bc.ca, Grade 2), which covers reading and writing together rather than as separate subjects. BC's explicit Grade 2 writing-related content is "letter formation" (legible printing with spacing), "sentence structure" (including compound sentences), and "conventions" (end punctuation and capitalization) plus general "writing processes" (revising, editing, considering audience) — it does not itemize specific grammar topics like "nouns/verbs/adjectives" the way this syllabus does. Those are a standard, developmentally appropriate breakdown used here for clarity and sequencing, not a mismatch with an official BC grade-level list the way multiplication was for Math.

## Strands and Big Ideas

Grade 2 writing grows across eight connected strands:

1. **Sentence Structure & Mechanics** — capitals, punctuation, complete sentences, and sentence types.
2. **Grammar & Parts of Speech** — nouns, verbs, adjectives, pronouns, and simple tense patterns.
3. **Paragraph Structure** — topic sentences, supporting details, and closing ideas.
4. **Narrative Writing** — personal recounts and simple stories with beginning, middle, and end.
5. **Opinion Writing** — stating an opinion, giving reasons, and using sentence frames when needed.
6. **Descriptive Writing** — adding sensory details and precise word choice.
7. **Handwriting & Presentation** — neat printing, spacing, margins, and readable final copies.
8. **The Writing Process** — planning, drafting, revising, editing, sharing, and reflecting.

Writing instruction should connect naturally to reading, oral language, and content learning. Students benefit from seeing strong mentor sentences and short model paragraphs while also having many chances to write their own ideas.

## Term 1 — Strong Sentences (September – November)

### 1. Sentence Basics
- Review what makes a complete sentence: a person/thing plus an action or state.
- Begin each sentence with a capital letter.
- End each sentence with correct punctuation: period, question mark, or exclamation mark.
- Tell the difference between a statement, question, exclamation, and command.
- Notice and fix simple fragments and run-on sentences.
- Build on Grade 1 sentence work and the earliest sentence frames introduced in reading.

📂 **Repo materials:** [Sentence Basics — index](01_sentence_basics/index.html) — 3 sheets: [Capitals & End Punctuation](01_sentence_basics/01_capitals_and_punctuation.html), [Sentence Types](01_sentence_basics/02_sentence_types.html), [Fragments & Run-Ons](01_sentence_basics/03_fragments_and_runons.html).

### 2. Handwriting, Spacing, and Presentation
- Form upper- and lower-case letters clearly and consistently. **(needs exercises)**
- Leave spaces between words. **(needs exercises)**
- Place writing neatly on ruled lines. **(needs exercises)**
- Copy a short sentence with correct spacing and punctuation. **(needs exercises)**
- Use an age-appropriate pencil grip and control. **(needs exercises)**

### 3. Oral Rehearsal Before Writing
- Say a sentence aloud before writing it. **(needs exercises)**
- Stretch an idea from one sentence to two or three related sentences. **(needs exercises)**
- Use prompts from shared reading or read-alouds as speaking-to-writing bridges. **(needs exercises)**
- Cross-reference story structure and comprehension conversations in `../reading/syllabus.md`. **(needs exercises)**

### 4. Early Writing Process Habits
- Draw or talk to plan before writing. **(needs exercises)**
- Re-read a sentence to check if it makes sense. **(needs exercises)**
- Check for capitals, spaces, and punctuation with a simple editing routine. **(needs exercises)**

## Term 2 — Grammar and Clear Meaning (December – February)

### 1. Nouns and Naming Words
- Identify nouns as naming words.
- Sort nouns by person, place, thing, and animal.
- Distinguish common nouns from proper nouns.
- Capitalize proper nouns such as names of people, schools, cities, months, and days.
- Make regular plurals with `-s` and `-es`.
- Recognize a few common irregular plurals such as *child → children*, *mouse → mice*, *tooth → teeth*. **(needs exercises)**

### 2. Verbs and Tense
- Identify action verbs in simple sentences.
- Choose present-tense and past-tense verbs that fit the meaning.
- Notice time clue words such as *today*, *yesterday*, and *last night*.
- Write original sentences using given verbs.
- Begin noticing helping verbs in simple patterns such as *is jumping* or *was playing*. **(needs exercises)**

### 3. Adjectives and Descriptive Language
- Identify adjectives as words that tell more about a noun.
- Add describing words to make plain sentences more interesting.
- Explore sensory adjectives for sight, sound, touch, taste, and smell.
- Compare a simple sentence to a more detailed one.
- Choose adjectives that match a topic rather than piling on random words.

📂 **Repo materials:** [Parts of Speech — index](02_parts_of_speech/index.html) — 3 sheets: [Nouns](02_parts_of_speech/01_nouns.html), [Verbs](02_parts_of_speech/02_verbs.html), [Adjectives](02_parts_of_speech/03_adjectives.html).

### 4. Pronouns (Introduction)
- Replace repeated nouns with simple pronouns such as *he, she, it, they, we*. **(needs exercises)**
- Match pronouns to the correct noun in context. **(needs exercises)**
- Revise awkward repeated sentences for smoother flow. **(needs exercises)**

### 5. Grammar Connections to Reading
- Notice capital letters, punctuation, and descriptive language inside decodable and connected texts. **(needs exercises)**
- Collect interesting verbs and adjectives from books. **(needs exercises)**
- Compare how a sentence sounds before and after revision. **(needs exercises)**
- Cross-reference sentence and comprehension work in `../reading/syllabus.md`. **(needs exercises)**

## Term 3 — Paragraph Building and Short Compositions (March – May)

### 1. Paragraph Structure
- Understand that a paragraph is a group of sentences about one main idea.
- Identify the topic sentence in a short model paragraph.
- Tell which sentences are supporting details.
- Add a simple closing sentence or wrap-up idea.
- Keep all sentences on the same topic.

📂 **Repo materials:** [Paragraph & Story Writing — index](03_paragraph_writing/index.html) — [Topic Sentences & Details](03_paragraph_writing/01_topic_sentences_and_details.html).

### 2. Narrative Writing
- Tell or write a personal recount in time order.
- Plan a beginning, middle, and end.
- Include who, where, and what happened.
- Add one or two feelings or reactions.
- Use time-order words such as *first*, *next*, *then*, and *finally*.

📂 **Repo materials:** [Narrative Story Prompt](03_paragraph_writing/02_narrative_story_prompt.html).

### 3. Opinion Writing
- State an opinion clearly.
- Give at least two reasons.
- Use sentence frames when needed, then move toward more independent writing.
- Use linking words such as *because*, *also*, and *so*.
- Build on Grade 1 opinion sentence frames such as “I like ___ best because ___,” but with longer Grade 2 responses.

📂 **Repo materials:** [Opinion Paragraph](03_paragraph_writing/03_opinion_paragraph.html).

### 4. Descriptive Writing
- Write about a familiar object, place, pet, or event using sensory details. **(needs exercises)**
- Choose specific nouns and vivid verbs. **(needs exercises)**
- Expand one sentence into two or three richer sentences. **(needs exercises)**
- Use adjectives that help the reader picture the scene. **(needs exercises)**

### 5. Revising for Clarity
- Re-read writing to add missing words or details. **(needs exercises)**
- Replace a weak word with a stronger one. **(needs exercises)**
- Check that each sentence stays on topic. **(needs exercises)**
- Use a peer or teacher conference to talk through improvements. **(needs exercises)**

## Term 4 — Publishing, Reflection, and Independence (June or final stretch)

### 1. Writing Process in Full
- Plan with a drawing, organizer, or oral rehearsal. **(needs exercises)**
- Draft a short piece without stopping for every error. **(needs exercises)**
- Revise by adding or improving details. **(needs exercises)**
- Edit for capitals, punctuation, spelling patterns, and neatness. **(needs exercises)**
- Publish a readable final copy by hand or on a template page. **(needs exercises)**

### 2. Spelling Within Writing
- Apply familiar phonics and word-study patterns while writing. **(needs exercises)**
- Use temporary spelling for unknown longer words while keeping ideas flowing. **(needs exercises)**
- Check high-frequency words from reading and classroom word walls. **(needs exercises)**
- Cross-reference phonics and sight-word expectations in `../reading/syllabus.md`. **(needs exercises)**

### 3. Sentence Variety at a Grade 2 Level
- Write a mix of statements, questions, commands, and exclamations for effect. **(needs exercises)**
- Combine two short ideas with *and*, *but*, or *so* when appropriate. **(needs exercises)**
- Avoid simple run-ons by hearing where one thought ends and another begins. **(needs exercises)**

### 4. Reflection and Sharing
- Read writing aloud to a partner or small group. **(needs exercises)**
- Notice one strength and one next step in a finished piece. **(needs exercises)**
- Celebrate growth from simple Grade 1-style sentence frames to fuller Grade 2 paragraphs and stories. **(needs exercises)**

## Year-End Expectations

By the end of Grade 2, most students should be able to:
- write complete sentences with capitals and end punctuation;
- choose the correct sentence type for a purpose;
- use simple grammar patterns involving nouns, verbs, adjectives, and basic pronouns;
- write a short paragraph with a topic sentence and supporting details;
- produce simple narrative, opinion, and descriptive pieces;
- plan, draft, revise, and edit with support;
- present writing neatly on lines so it is easy to read.
