# Grade 2 Reading — Comprehensive Syllabus

A complete year-long reading syllabus aimed at a Canadian Grade 2 level (ages 7–8). It builds on Grade 1's phonics, sight-word, and fluency foundation and extends the learner into multisyllabic decoding, morphology, richer vocabulary, longer texts, and deeper comprehension. Links to existing repo sheets are included as 📂 references; missing materials are marked **(needs exercises)** so they can be filled in later.

The school year is divided into four terms. Pacing is flexible — slow down, repeat, or skip as needed.

> **Curriculum alignment note:** Cross-checked against the official **BC Ministry of Education English Language Arts curriculum** (curriculum.gov.bc.ca, Grade 2). Unlike Math, BC's ELA curriculum for Grade 2 is holistic and competency-based rather than a checklist of named topics — its official content areas are broadly "Story/text elements" (including literary elements, poetic language, and figurative language), "Strategies and processes" (reading, **oral language**, metacognitive, writing), and "Language features, structures, and conventions" (word patterns/families, letter formation, sentence structure including compound sentences, punctuation, and capitalization). It does not name specific sub-skills like "syllable types," "prefixes and suffixes," or "rhyme and alliteration" the way this syllabus does — those are a standard, developmentally appropriate breakdown of BC's strands used here for clarity and sequencing, not a mismatch with an official BC grade-level list the way multiplication was for Math. BC's curricular competencies also explicitly call for **listening and speaking** ("comprehend and connect... reading, listening, viewing" / "create and communicate... writing, speaking, representing") and ask students to **show awareness of how story in First Peoples cultures connects people to family and community** — strands 8–9 below (Poetry & Wordplay, Oral Storytelling & Listening) build out this official but previously under-represented content.

## Strands and Big Ideas

Grade 2 reading is built on nine interlocking strands:

1. **Word Study & Decoding** — reading longer words using syllable types, syllable division, and known spelling patterns.
2. **Morphology & Word Meaning** — using prefixes, suffixes, and base words to unlock new vocabulary.
3. **High-Frequency / Sight Words** — rapidly recognizing common words in connected text.
4. **Fluency** — reading with accuracy, rate, phrasing, and expression across longer passages.
5. **Vocabulary** — learning new words through context, discussion, and word relationships.
6. **Comprehension** — understanding fiction and nonfiction texts, asking questions, and using evidence.
7. **Writing Connection** — responding to reading in sentences, paragraphs, and short organized pieces.
8. **Literary Appreciation & Poetic Language** — rhyme, alliteration, onomatopoeia, and comparisons that make writing playful and vivid (BC Grade 2 core: "Story/text" literary elements and poetic language).
9. **Oral Language & Story Traditions** — active listening, speaking with expression, and the many purposes of oral storytelling, including First Peoples storytelling traditions (BC Grade 2 core: "Strategies and processes" oral language; "Comprehend and connect" listening).

Each term below revisits multiple strands so learning spirals through the year.

## Term 1 — Strengthening the Foundation (September – November)

### 1. Multisyllabic Word Decoding & Syllable Types
- Review and apply **closed syllables** (short vowel sound: *nap-kin, rab-bit*).
- Review and apply **open syllables** (long vowel sound: *ba-by, ti-ger*).
- Introduce **VCe / silent-e syllables** in longer words: *in-side, ex-cuse*. 
- Introduce common **vowel-team syllables**: *rain-bow, sea-son, paint-er*.
- Read and sort words by syllable type. 
- Begin dividing words with the **VC/CV** pattern (divide between two middle consonants): *rab-bit, pic-nic, sun-set*.

📂 **Repo materials:** [Syllable Types & Division — index](01_syllable_types/index.html) — [Closed & Open Syllables](01_syllable_types/01_closed_open_syllables.html), [Silent-e & Vowel-Team Syllables](01_syllable_types/02_silent_e_vowel_team_syllables.html), [Dividing Multisyllabic Words](01_syllable_types/03_dividing_multisyllabic_words.html).

### 2. Prefixes, Suffixes & Morphology
- Learn that a **prefix** comes at the beginning of a word and changes meaning.
- Learn that a **suffix** comes at the end of a word and changes meaning or function.
- Read and discuss common prefixes: **un-, re-, pre-, dis-**.
- Read and discuss common suffixes: **-ful, -less, -ly, -er, -est**.
- Build new words from known base words. 

📂 **Repo materials:** [Prefixes & Suffixes — index](02_prefixes_suffixes/index.html) — [Common Prefixes](02_prefixes_suffixes/01_common_prefixes.html), [Common Suffixes](02_prefixes_suffixes/02_common_suffixes.html), [Mixed Prefix & Suffix Practice](02_prefixes_suffixes/03_mixed_prefix_suffix_practice.html).

### 3. High-Frequency Vocabulary
- Review Grade 1 high-frequency words in connected text.
- Add expanded Grade 2 words such as: because, before, after, every, another, people, around, answer, again, against, above, below, thought, enough, always. **(needs exercises)**
- Notice irregular spellings in common words. **(needs exercises)**

### 4. Fluency
- Read short passages of 60–100 words with attention to accuracy and phrasing.
- Scoop words into meaningful phrases instead of reading one word at a time.
- Use punctuation and dialogue clues to guide expression.
- Re-read passages to improve smoothness and confidence. **(needs exercises)**

### 5. Comprehension Strategies
- Identify the **main idea** and supporting details in short nonfiction passages.
- Retell events in order using *first, next, then, last*.
- Ask and answer literal questions using evidence from the text.
- Notice whether a text is **fiction** or **nonfiction**. 

📂 **Repo materials:** [Comprehension — index](03_comprehension/index.html) — [Main Idea & Details](03_comprehension/01_main_idea_details.html), [Story Sequencing](03_comprehension/02_story_sequencing.html), [Fiction vs. Nonfiction](03_comprehension/03_fiction_vs_nonfiction.html).

### 6. Vocabulary Building
- Use picture clues, nearby words, and sentence meaning to figure out unfamiliar vocabulary.
- Sort words into categories and discuss shades of meaning. **(needs exercises)**
- Build oral vocabulary through read-alouds, discussion, and content topics. **(needs exercises)**

### 7. Writing Connection
- Write 2–4 connected sentences about a text using a capital letter and end punctuation.
- Answer a reading question with a complete sentence.
- Begin using simple evidence language such as “I know this because…”. **(needs exercises)**
- Cross-reference developing composition skills in [Writing Syllabus](../writing/syllabus.md). **(needs exercises)**

## Term 2 — Reading Bigger Words and Richer Texts (December – February)

### 1. Syllable Patterns & Flexible Decoding
- Practice **closed/open/VCe/vowel-team** syllables in longer words.
- Read 2-syllable and some 3-syllable words by chunking into syllables.
- Apply VC/CV, V/CV, and VC/V patterns where appropriate. **(needs exercises)**
- Use known syllable types to self-correct when a word does not sound right. **(needs exercises)**

### 2. Morphology & Word Families
- Notice how prefixes change meaning: *happy → unhappy*, *heat → reheat*.
- Notice how suffixes change meaning or grammar: *hope → hopeful*, *quick → quickly*, *tall → taller*.
- Compare related words in a word family. **(needs exercises)**
- Begin discussing root/base words in simple terms. **(needs exercises)**

### 3. High-Frequency Vocabulary
- Expand automatic recognition of common Grade 2 words in stories and informational text. **(needs exercises)**
- Study common academic and classroom words such as *answer, example, question, information, important*. **(needs exercises)**

### 4. Fluency
- Read passages of 80–120 words with improved pace and expression.
- Read dialogue with attention to speaker voice.
- Monitor for meaning and re-read when a sentence does not make sense. **(needs exercises)**

### 5. Comprehension Strategies
- Compare and contrast two texts, characters, or topics.
- Identify **cause and effect** in everyday reading. **(needs exercises)**
- Make simple **inferences** using clues plus background knowledge. **(needs exercises)**
- Distinguish important details from extra details. **(needs exercises)**

### 6. Vocabulary Building
- Use context clues to choose the best meaning of a word. **(needs exercises)**
- Learn synonyms, antonyms, and multiple-meaning words. **(needs exercises)**

### 7. Writing Connection
- Write a retelling with beginning, middle, and end.
- Write a short response comparing two ideas from reading. **(needs exercises)**
- Connect reading responses to paragraph-building work in [Writing Syllabus](../writing/syllabus.md). **(needs exercises)**

## Term 3 — Thinking More Deeply About Text (March – May)

### 1. Advanced Word Solving
- Apply syllable knowledge to unfamiliar science, social studies, and story vocabulary.
- Recognize more vowel teams and stable syllable chunks in longer words. **(needs exercises)**
- Use morphology to decode and understand words with familiar prefixes and suffixes. **(needs exercises)**

### 2. Fluency
- Read 100–150 word passages with strong accuracy and expression.
- Pause at commas, question marks, and quotation marks appropriately. **(needs exercises)**
- Practice partner reading and echo reading. **(needs exercises)**

### 3. Fiction Comprehension
- Identify characters, setting, problem, events, and solution in longer stories. **(needs exercises)**
- Infer how a character feels using text clues. **(needs exercises)**
- Compare two versions of a familiar story. **(needs exercises)**

### 4. Nonfiction Comprehension
- Identify **main idea** and several supporting details.
- Recognize nonfiction text features: title, heading, caption, bold word, label, diagram, table of contents.
- Read to learn facts and explain what was learned. **(needs exercises)**

### 5. Vocabulary Building
- Track new words from reading in oral discussion and notebook work. **(needs exercises)**
- Learn content vocabulary connected to science, community, nature, and health topics. **(needs exercises)**

### 6. Writing Connection
- Write a short informational paragraph using facts from reading.
- Write an opinion about a text and support it with a reason.
- Build toward organized paragraphs and simple reports described in [Writing Syllabus](../writing/syllabus.md). **(needs exercises)**

## Term 4 — Independent Grade 2 Readers (June)

### 1. Review & Consolidation
- Review all Grade 2 syllable types and division strategies.
- Review common prefixes, suffixes, and related word meanings.
- Review Grade 1 and Grade 2 high-frequency words in connected text.
- Re-read favourite stories and nonfiction texts for fluency and confidence.

### 2. Reading Behaviours
- Choose “just-right” books independently.
- Sustain independent reading for 20 minutes.
- Use a flexible toolkit when stuck: chunk the word, look for a prefix/suffix, re-read, and think about meaning.
- Talk about books with a partner using clear reasons and examples.

### 3. Comprehension Across Texts
- Compare fiction and nonfiction texts on a shared topic.
- Explain main idea, details, sequence, cause/effect, and simple inference when appropriate. **(needs exercises)**
- Use evidence language such as “The text says…” and “I think… because…”. **(needs exercises)**

### 4. Literary Appreciation: Poetry & Wordplay *(BC Grade 2 core)*
- Notice and create rhyme, alliteration, and onomatopoeia in short poems and sentences.
- Use simple comparisons ("as ___ as ___", "like a ___") to describe things in a fresh, vivid way.
- Talk about how word choice and sound play affect how a poem feels.

📂 **Repo materials:** [Poetry & Wordplay — index](04_poetry_and_wordplay/index.html) — [Rhyming Words](04_poetry_and_wordplay/01_rhyming_words.html), [Alliteration & Onomatopoeia](04_poetry_and_wordplay/02_alliteration_and_onomatopoeia.html), [Comparing With Words: Similes](04_poetry_and_wordplay/03_comparing_with_words_similes.html).

### 5. Oral Language: Storytelling & Listening *(BC Grade 2 core)*
- Practice active listening: eyes on the speaker, asking clarifying questions, and taking turns in conversation.
- Use tone, volume, pace, and gestures to tell a familiar story out loud with expression.
- Learn that stories have long been passed down out loud to teach, share history, explain the world, and entertain — including First Peoples oral storytelling traditions in BC, where each Nation's stories belong to that Nation and its families.

📂 **Repo materials:** [Oral Storytelling & Listening — index](05_oral_storytelling_and_listening/index.html) — [Listening & Discussion Skills](05_oral_storytelling_and_listening/01_listening_and_discussion_skills.html), [Telling a Story Out Loud](05_oral_storytelling_and_listening/02_telling_a_story_out_loud.html), [Stories Passed Down: Oral Storytelling Traditions](05_oral_storytelling_and_listening/03_stories_passed_down.html).

### 6. Writing Connection
- Write a short story response, informational response, or opinion response based on reading.
- Revise for capitals, punctuation, spacing, and clearer word choice.
- Link independent reading responses to longer writing pieces in [Writing Syllabus](../writing/syllabus.md). **(needs exercises)**

## End-of-Year Goals

By the end of Grade 2, the learner should be able to:

**Word Study & Decoding**
- Decode many two-syllable words using closed, open, VCe, and vowel-team syllable knowledge.
- Divide common multisyllabic words using familiar patterns such as VC/CV.
- Use prefixes and suffixes to read and understand related words.

**High-Frequency Vocabulary**
- Read a broad bank of Grade 1 and Grade 2 high-frequency words automatically in connected text.

**Fluency**
- Read Grade 2 text accurately, smoothly, and with expression at an age-appropriate pace.

**Comprehension**
- Identify main idea and important details.
- Retell events in order and use sequencing vocabulary.
- Compare and contrast texts or ideas.
- Distinguish fiction from nonfiction and identify basic text features.
- Make simple inferences and explain thinking using text clues.

**Vocabulary & Habits**
- Use context clues and word parts to understand unfamiliar words.
- Read independently for enjoyment and learning for 20+ minutes a day.

**Literary Appreciation**
- Recognize and create rhyme, alliteration, onomatopoeia, and simple comparisons ("as ___ as ___", "like a ___") in poems and sentences.

**Oral Language**
- Listen actively, ask clarifying questions, and take turns in discussion.
- Tell a familiar story out loud with clear expression, using tone, volume, pace, and gestures.
- Understand that oral storytelling has long served to teach, share history, explain the world, and entertain, including First Peoples traditions in BC.
