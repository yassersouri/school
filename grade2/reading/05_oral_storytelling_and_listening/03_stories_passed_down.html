1) Read Along 📖
Telling Stories Out Loud Long before there were books, people shared stories by speaking them out loud. A grandparent, parent, or Elder would tell a story to children, who would remember it and tell it to their own children one day. This is called oral storytelling — "oral" means spoken.

First Peoples across British Columbia have told stories out loud for thousands of years. Each Nation has its own stories, and they belong to that Nation and its families. These stories can teach lessons, share history, explain the world, and bring people together. Families all over the world — from every culture — pass down stories the very same way.

Match each example to the reason people might tell that story.

Example

  • A story warns children to be careful near rivers.
  • A grandmother tells what life was like when she was young.
  • A funny story makes everyone laugh around the table.
  • A story explains why the seasons change.

Reason

  • Explaining the world
  • Teaching a lesson
  • Sharing family history
  • Entertaining people

Note for grown-ups: First Peoples communities in BC each have their own distinct oral storytelling traditions, and their stories belong to them. This sheet intentionally focuses on the general purposes of oral storytelling rather than retelling any specific story. To explore authentic stories, invite a local knowledge keeper or Elder to share, or look to trusted published sources such as your local First Nation's website or the First Nations Education Steering Committee (FNESC).
